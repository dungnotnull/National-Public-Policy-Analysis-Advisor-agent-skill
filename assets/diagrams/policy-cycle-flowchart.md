## Key Dynamics

### Feedback Loops

1. **Evaluation → Agenda Setting**
   - Evaluation findings identify new problems
   - Failed policies return to agenda
   - Successful policies expand to new areas

2. **Implementation → Formulation**
   - Implementation problems trigger policy redesign
   - Practical experience informs policy refinement

3. **Decision → Formulation**
   - Political defeat prompts reformulation
   - Compromise requirements shape alternatives

## Non-Linearity

```
┌─────────────┐
│   Agenda    │
└──────┬──────┘
       │
       ├─► [Fast Track] ─► Decision ◄─────┘
       │                                (Emergency action)
       │
       └─► [Standard Path] ─► Formulation ─► Decision ─► ...
                                      (Skip if urgent)
```

**Reality:**
- Stages often occur simultaneously
- Can skip stages in emergencies
- Can return to earlier stages
- Multiple issues can progress in parallel
- International processes add complexity

## Key Takeaways

1. **Non-linear**: Real policy processes don't follow neat stages
2. **Iterative**: Feedback loops return issues to earlier stages
3. **Political**: Power and politics shape every stage
4. **Contextual**: Institutions, economics, and culture matter
5. **Gap**: Implementation often diverges from design
6. **Multiple Streams**: Problems, policies, and politics must converge

## Application Notes

When analyzing policy issues:

1. **Identify current stage**: Where is the policy in the cycle?
2. **Map constraints**: What limits movement through stages?
3. **Analyze politics**: Who supports/opposes at each stage?
4. **Assess capacity**: What resources are needed/available?
5. **Consider feedback**: How might evaluation reshape agenda?
6. **Acknowledge context**: How do institutions and conditions shape the process?
