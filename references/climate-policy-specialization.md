# Climate Policy Specialization Framework

## Research Foundation

This specialized framework integrates climate science, economics, and policy for comprehensive climate policy analysis, drawing from:

- **IPCC (2014-2023)** - Assessment Reports 5-6
- **Nordhaus (2015)** - Climate Casino: Risk, Uncertainty, and Economics
- **Stern (2007)** - Economics of Climate Change
- **Aldy & Stavins (2012)** - Climate Policy Architecture
- **Keohane & Victor (2016)** - Climate Politics
- **Pizer (2019)** - Designing Climate Policy
- **World Bank (2021)** - State and Trends of Carbon Pricing

## Climate-Specific Problem Definition

### Climate Problem Dimensions

#### 1. Global Commons Problem

**Characteristics:**
- No ownership of atmosphere
- Free-rider problem in mitigation
- Collective action challenges
- Intergenerational equity issues

**Policy Implications:**
- International coordination essential
- National sovereignty tensions
- Benefit-cost calculation complexities
- Intergenerational discounting debates

#### 2. Thresholds and Tipping Points

**Climate Tipping Points:**
- Ice sheet collapse (Greenland, Antarctica)
- Amazon dieback
- Permafrost methane release
- Coral reef ecosystem collapse
- Atlantic circulation slowdown

**Policy Implications:**
- Precautionary principle application
- Tail-risk consideration
- Irreversibility implications
- Option value in mitigation

#### 3. Multi-Dimensional Impacts

**Impact Categories:**
- Temperature extremes
- Precipitation changes
- Sea level rise
- Ocean acidification
- Ecosystem disruption
- Human health impacts
- Economic damages
- Social disruption

**Policy Implications:**
- Multi-criteria analysis essential
- Distributional considerations central
- Adaptation-Mitigation tradeoffs
- Sector-specific vulnerabilities

### Climate Problem Framing Analysis

**Competing Framings:**

**Economic Efficiency Framing:**
- Problem: Market failure in carbon pricing
- Solution: Cost-effective carbon pricing
- Stakeholders: Economists, business groups
- Metrics: Cost per ton abated

**Moral/Justice Framing:**
- Problem: Historical responsibility, climate injustice
- Solution: Equity-based allocation
- Stakeholders: Vulnerable nations, justice advocates
- Metrics: Per capita emissions, historical responsibility

**Security/Stability Framing:**
- Problem: Climate threat to security and stability
- Solution: Resilience and threat reduction
- Stakeholders: Security agencies, military
- Metrics: Conflict risk, migration pressures

**Technological Optimism Framing:**
- Problem: Innovation and deployment barriers
- Solution: Technology-push policies
- Stakeholders: Clean tech industry, innovation advocates
- Metrics: R&D spending, deployment rates

**Emergency/Crisis Framing:**
- Problem: Existential climate emergency
- Solution: Rapid transformation
- Stakeholders: Climate activists, youth movements
- Metrics: Emission reduction urgency

## Climate Policy Alternatives

### Carbon Pricing Instruments

#### 1. Carbon Taxes

**Design Dimensions:**
- Tax rate level
- Coverage (sectors, gases)
- Point of regulation
- Border adjustments
- Revenue recycling

**Advantages:**
- Price certainty
- Administrative simplicity
- Revenue generation
- Innovation incentives

**Challenges:**
- Political acceptability
- Distributional impacts
- Competitiveness concerns
- Emission uncertainty

**Evidence:**
- British Columbia carbon tax (broad acceptance, emission reductions)
- Sweden carbon tax (high rates, economic growth maintained)
- France carbon tax (political backlash, Yellow Vest protests)

#### 2. Cap-and-Trade Systems

**Design Dimensions:**
- Emission caps (level, trajectory)
- Allowance allocation (auction vs. free)
- Price floors and ceilings
- Banking and borrowing
- Offset provisions

**Advantages:**
- Emission certainty
- Cost-effectiveness
- International linking
- Innovation incentives

**Challenges:**
- Price volatility
- Complexity
- Distributional allocation
- Market manipulation risks

**Evidence:**
- EU ETS (evolution, learning, price stability improvements)
- California-Quebec linkage (international cooperation)
- RGGI (successful regional implementation)

#### 3. Hybrid Systems

**Design Features:**
- Carbon tax with price ceiling
- Cap-and-trade with price floor
- Tax with intensity-based rebates
- Mixed approaches

**Evidence:**
- Canada (federal backstop hybrid system)
- China (ETS with price management)

## Advanced Climate Analysis Tools

### Carbon Budget Analysis

**Methodology:**
1. Define temperature target (1.5°C, 2°C)
2. Calculate remaining carbon budget
3. Allocate budget (global, national, sectoral)
4. Assess current trajectory
5. Identify required changes
6. Scenario analysis for pathways

**Tools:**
- Carbon budget calculators
- Integrated assessment models (IAMs)
- Emissions trajectory models
- Technology pathway models

### Transition Pathway Analysis

**Components:**
- Emission reduction pathways
- Technology deployment scenarios
- Investment requirements
- Infrastructure transformation
- Institutional adaptation

**Methods:**
- Backcasting from targets
- Scenario analysis
- Cross-sectoral modeling
- International coordination modeling

- Transition risks (policy, technology, market)
- Physical risks (acute, chronic)
- Liability risks
- Reputational risks

**Assessment Framework:**
- Risk identification
